# K–12 Math Topics & Learning Focus by Stage — Detailed

A stage-by-stage breakdown of the US math progression, organized by sub-strand, with the **core focus**, granular skills, and an end-of-stage **mastery checkpoint** for each. Grades 3–5 are split into a **Basics** tier (the must-master floor) and an **Advanced** tier (the stretch that bridges into middle school).

**The throughline:**
Fractions → Ratios & Proportions → Linear Functions → Functions (all kinds) → Calculus

---

## 1. Grades 3–5 · Basics — Core: **Whole-Number Fluency & Fraction Foundations**

**Operations & Algebraic Thinking**
- Interpret multiplication (equal groups, arrays, area) and division (sharing, measurement)
- Multiplication & division facts within 100 — fluency by the end of Grade 3
- Properties of operations: commutative, associative, distributive
- One- and two-step word problems with the four operations; estimate and check reasonableness

**Number & Operations in Base Ten**
- Place value to thousands, then to millions; read, write, and compare
- Round to the nearest 10, 100, 1,000
- Fluent multi-digit addition & subtraction (standard algorithm by Grade 4)

**Fractions — Foundations**
- Understand a fraction as a number on the number line
- Unit fractions; build other fractions from unit fractions
- Equivalent fractions with visual models; compare fractions with the same numerator or denominator

**Intro to Decimals**
- Tenths and hundredths as both fractions and decimals
- Connect decimals to money and measurement

**Measurement, Data & Geometry**
- Time to the minute and elapsed time; mass and liquid volume
- Area by tiling with unit squares; perimeter
- Picture graphs, bar graphs, and line plots
- Classify shapes by attributes (sides, angles); partition shapes into equal parts

**Mastery checkpoint:** fluent with multiplication/division facts and multi-digit addition/subtraction, and understands fractions as quantities.

→ *Leads to fraction & decimal operations.*

## 2. Grades 3–5 · Advanced — Core: **Fraction & Decimal Operations**

**Fractions — Operations**
- Add & subtract fractions with unlike denominators (find a common denominator) — Grade 5
- Add & subtract mixed numbers, with regrouping
- Multiply a fraction by a whole number (Grade 4); multiply a fraction by a fraction (Grade 5)
- Divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions (Grade 5)
- Interpret a fraction as division (a/b = a ÷ b)

**Decimals — Operations & Place Value**
- Place value to thousandths; read, write, compare, and round decimals
- Add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals
- Connect decimal operations back to fraction reasoning

**Operations & Algebraic Thinking**
- Multi-step, multi-operation word problems; interpret remainders in context
- Factors and multiples; prime and composite numbers (Grade 4)
- Generate and analyze numerical patterns; relate two patterns

**Geometry & Measurement**
- Volume as cubic units; V = l × w × h (Grade 5)
- Coordinate plane: plot and interpret points in the first quadrant
- Classify 2-D figures in a hierarchy (e.g., every square is a rectangle)
- Measure angles with a protractor; add adjacent angles
- Convert units within a measurement system; line plots with fractional units

**Mastery checkpoint:** operates fluently with fractions and decimals, solves multi-step problems, and reasons about volume and the coordinate plane.

→ *Bridges into ratios & proportions.*

## 3. Grades 6–7 · Early Middle School — Core: **Ratios & Proportions**

**Ratios & Proportional Relationships**
- Ratio language; equivalent ratios with tables and double number lines
- Unit rates; rate problems (speed, unit price)
- Percent as a rate per 100; percent problems (Grade 6)
- Identify proportional relationships; constant of proportionality *k*; y = kx (Grade 7)
- Multi-step percent applications: tax, tip, discount, markup, simple interest (Grade 7)
- Scale drawings and scale factor

**The Number System**
- Divide fractions by fractions and interpret the quotient (Grade 6)
- Negative numbers: number line, opposites, absolute value (Grade 6)
- All four operations with rational numbers, including negatives (Grade 7)
- Greatest common factor, least common multiple; distributive property with the GCF

**Expressions & Equations**
- Write, read, and evaluate expressions using variables
- Equivalent expressions: combine like terms, distribute, factor
- Solve one-variable equations (px + q = r) and inequalities
- Dependent vs independent variables; relationships in tables and graphs

**Geometry**
- Area of triangles, quadrilaterals, and polygons by decomposition
- Surface area from nets; volume with fractional edge lengths
- Angle relationships: supplementary, complementary, vertical, adjacent
- Circles: circumference and area (Grade 7)

**Statistics & Probability**
- Statistical questions and distributions; center (mean, median) and variability (range, IQR, MAD)
- Random sampling and inferences about a population (Grade 7)
- Compare two populations
- Probability: theoretical vs experimental; compound events and simulations (Grade 7)

**Mastery checkpoint:** reasons proportionally, operates with all rational numbers, and solves linear equations.

→ *Constant of proportionality → slope, which leads into functions.*

## 4. Grade 8 · Pre-Algebra — Core: **Linear Functions**

**The Number System**
- Rational vs irrational numbers; decimal expansions
- Approximate irrational numbers (e.g., locate √2 on a number line)

**Expressions & Equations**
- Integer-exponent properties; square and cube roots
- Scientific notation and operations with it
- Slope and proportional relationships; compare relationships across representations
- Solve linear equations in one variable (including no-solution and infinitely-many cases)
- Systems of two linear equations: by graphing, substitution, and elimination

**Functions**
- Define a function (one output per input); recognize functions from tables, graphs, equations
- Compare functions in different representations
- Linear functions: rate of change (slope) and initial value (intercept)
- Model relationships; describe graphs qualitatively (increasing/decreasing, linear/nonlinear)

**Geometry**
- Transformations: translations, rotations, reflections, dilations
- Congruence and similarity defined through transformations
- Angles with parallel lines and a transversal; angle-angle similarity
- Pythagorean theorem: informal proof, applications, distance between two points
- Volume of cylinders, cones, and spheres

**Statistics & Probability**
- Bivariate data and scatter plots: clustering, outliers, association
- Fit a line and interpret slope and intercept
- Two-way tables for categorical data

**Mastery checkpoint:** understands functions, fluently handles linear equations and systems, and applies the Pythagorean theorem.

→ *Bridges into high school Algebra I.*

## 5. Algebra I · ~Grade 9 — Core: **Quadratic Functions**

- Linear equations, inequalities, and systems (deepened from Grade 8)
- Functions: domain and range, function notation, evaluating; sequences as functions
- Linear and exponential functions: build, interpret, and compare growth
- Exponent rules and radicals; polynomial operations (add, subtract, multiply, factor)
- Quadratic functions: graph features (vertex, intercepts, axis of symmetry), transformations
- Solving quadratics: factoring, completing the square, the quadratic formula, the discriminant
- Modeling with linear, quadratic, and exponential functions
- Descriptive statistics: dot plots, histograms, box plots, mean and standard deviation; scatter plots, lines of best fit, correlation vs causation

**Mastery checkpoint:** models and solves problems with linear, exponential, and quadratic functions.

→ *The foundation of high school algebra.*

## 6. Geometry · ~Grade 10 — Core: **Proof & Trigonometry**

- Definitions, postulates, and the structure of deductive proof (two-column, paragraph, flowchart)
- Congruence via rigid motions; triangle congruence (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL)
- Similarity via dilations; similar triangles (AA); proportional reasoning in figures
- Right-triangle trigonometry: sine, cosine, tangent; solving right triangles; angles of elevation/depression
- Pythagorean theorem and its converse; special right triangles (30-60-90, 45-45-90)
- Circles: central and inscribed angles, arcs, chords, tangents, sector area, arc length; equations of circles
- Coordinate (analytic) geometry: distance, midpoint, slope; proving properties of figures
- Measurement: area, surface area, volume; cross-sections and solids of revolution
- (Often) basic probability: independence and conditional probability

**Mastery checkpoint:** constructs logical proofs and applies right-triangle trigonometry.

→ *Trains deductive reasoning.*

## 7. Algebra II · ~Grade 11 — Core: **Exponentials, Logs & Polynomials**

- Polynomial functions: end behavior, zeros and multiplicity; Remainder & Factor theorems; polynomial division
- Complex numbers; the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra
- Rational expressions and functions; asymptotes; solving rational equations
- Radical functions and equations; rational exponents
- Exponential & logarithmic functions: properties of logarithms, solving equations, modeling growth and decay
- Trigonometric functions: unit circle, radian measure, graphs of sine/cosine/tangent, basic identities
- Sequences and series: arithmetic and geometric; summation
- Statistics: normal distribution and z-scores; sampling and an introduction to inference; simulations

**Mastery checkpoint:** works fluently across polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, and logarithmic functions.

→ *A major expansion of the function toolbox.*

## 8. Precalculus · Grades 11–12 — Core: **Trigonometry & Intro Limits**

- Functions in depth: composition, inverses, transformations, piecewise; analyzing all function families
- Trigonometry: full unit circle, graphs and transformations, identities (Pythagorean, sum/difference, double-angle), solving trig equations, inverse trig functions
- Law of Sines and Law of Cosines; vectors (components and operations)
- Matrices: operations, solving systems, determinants
- Polar coordinates and parametric equations; complex numbers in polar form
- Conic sections: parabola, ellipse, hyperbola
- Sequences and series, including the binomial theorem
- Introduction to limits and continuity (preparing for calculus)
- (AP Precalculus, launched 2023, formalizes much of this content)

**Mastery checkpoint:** commands trigonometry and function analysis, and is ready for the concept of a limit.

→ *Paves the way for calculus.*

## 9. Calculus / Statistics · Grade 12 · AP — Core: **Calculus / Statistical Inference**

**AP Calculus AB / BC**
- Limits and continuity; the formal idea of approaching a value
- Derivatives: definition; rules (power, product, quotient, chain); implicit differentiation
- Applications of derivatives: rates of change, optimization, related rates, curve analysis
- Integrals: antiderivatives, the definite integral, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
- Applications of integration: area, volume, accumulation
- *BC adds:* integration techniques, parametric/polar/vector functions, infinite sequences & series (Taylor/Maclaurin)

**AP Statistics**
- Exploring data: distributions, summary statistics, graphical displays
- Sampling and experimental design; sources of bias
- Probability, random variables, and distributions (binomial, normal)
- Inference: confidence intervals and hypothesis tests for means and proportions; chi-square; regression inference

**Mastery checkpoint:** reasons about change and accumulation (calculus), or about data and uncertainty (statistics).

→ *Leads into college mathematics.*

---

## Notes on structure

- **Why split 3–5.** *Basics* is the non-negotiable floor (fact fluency, fraction concepts); *Advanced* is the upper-end operations and multi-step problem solving that prepare students for Grade 6 ratios. The split lets you place a student precisely rather than by grade label alone.
- **Two pathways.** Traditional (most schools): Algebra I → Geometry → Algebra II → (Precalculus) → (Calculus / Statistics). Integrated: Math I / II / III, blending strands each year.
- **Starting point varies.** A typical student takes Algebra I in Grade 9; advanced students take it in Grade 8 and can reach calculus in high school.
- **College expectations.** Most colleges expect at least through Algebra II; STEM and competitive programs expect Precalculus plus Calculus (AP).
- **Spiral, don't silo.** Because math is cumulative, each stage's topics should be revisited inside later stages — fractions resurface in ratios, slope in linear functions, functions in calculus.

*Fractions → Ratios & Proportions → Linear Functions → Functions → Calculus — each stage is the groundwork for the next.*
